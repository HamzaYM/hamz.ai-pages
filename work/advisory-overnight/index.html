The CRM stopped being a database and started acting

An independent capital-markets advisory firm helps public companies increase their market valuation. Its business-development team tracks roughly 7,000 companies in Dynamics 365, and the moments that matter, an acquisition, an earnings event, kept surfacing weeks after the best pitch window had closed. I made the CRM act on those moments: every event gets an owner and a deadline that cannot silently expire, and AI-drafted outreach waits behind explicit human approval.

01The problem

A CRM that stored everything and acted on nothing

Three full-time BD people, plus a CEO and partners who actively pitch, track roughly 7,000 companies in a Dynamics 365 Sales Hub environment. It had been heavily customized over several years by one person, with no formal documentation and zero automation. The data went in. Nothing ever came back out at the right moment.

The cost showed up as missed triggers. A prospect makes an acquisition or reports earnings, and the team hears about it weeks later, after the moment when a pitch would have landed. Around that sat manual everything: emails logged by hand, meeting notes typed up by hand, market data uploaded by hand with the columns matched one at a time. People ran consumer AI chatbots on the side and pasted the results into emails, where nothing was stored and nothing could be reused.

02The approach

Discovery first: know the environment before automating it

You cannot safely automate a system nobody has mapped. Before building anything, I inventoried every custom table, field, workflow, Power Automate flow, plugin, and integration in the environment, and shadowed the BD team through their real day-to-day rather than the workflow as anyone described it from memory.

From that came the ground rules: data-freshness standards, clear ownership of each record type, and baseline follow-up metrics captured before any automation existed, so later improvement claims would have something real to stand on. The client and I also agreed on the design principles up front, and every later decision was checked against them.

Automation reduces friction, never adds administrative burden
If a change makes the team do more admin than before, it does not ship.
02

A current record the team trusts beats a full record nobody believes. Staleness is treated as a defect.

The system's job is to put the next step in front of the right person while the moment is still open.

Incremental, with value early
Each phase had to be useful on its own before the next one started.
03What I built

Events get owners, owners get drafts, nothing expires quietly

The core of the build is a standardized Company Event table: earnings releases, investor days, and management changes, populated by FactSet imports and manual inputs. When an event lands, automation assigns an owner, sets a status, and notifies on upcoming and overdue events. An event cannot silently expire. It is either acted on or explicitly closed, and the record shows which.

THE GATE
Human review
Nothing sends without explicit approval. The event closes with an action or an explicit decision, never by timing out.

Around the event flow sit the pieces that make the drafts worth reading. Meetings produce a structured Meeting Insight record, linked to the appointment and its tasks: a summary, key themes, personalization cues, and next steps, with automated prompts to refresh insights that have gone stale. Introductory meetings generate follow-up tasks automatically, with service-level expectations, escalation rules, and tracked completion and outcomes. The outreach drafts are assembled from those insights, the extracted document text, and selected marketing modules, with different templates and prompting logic per trigger type.

The event gets an owner. The owner gets a draft. Nothing sends itself.
04The roadmap

Seven phases, each useful before the next started

The work was scoped as seven phases, ordered so that every phase delivered something the team used before the next one began. All seven shipped.

  1. Discovery and foundation. Full environment inventory, the BD workflow documented end to end from real examples, data-freshness standards, and baseline follow-up metrics.
  2. Company-event and trigger automation. The standardized Company Event table, fed by FactSet imports and manual inputs, with owners, statuses, notifications, and no silent expiry.
  3. Document management and content automation. Standardized SharePoint storage linked to accounts and events, required metadata at upload, and automated extraction of usable text from earnings releases for downstream AI and reporting.
  4. Meeting-insight structuring. A structured Meeting Insight record linked to appointments and tasks: summaries, key themes, personalization cues, next steps, with automated prompts to refresh stale insights.
  5. Follow-up automation. Automatic follow-up tasks after introductory meetings, service-level expectations, escalation rules, and tracked completion and outcomes.
  6. AI-drafted outreach. Follow-up emails drafted from structured meeting insights, extracted document content, and selected marketing modules, with different templates and prompting logic per trigger type, behind human review and approval.
  7. Measurement and continuous improvement. Automation adoption, follow-up timing, draft usage and edit rates, and conversion outcomes tracked, with rules, templates, and prompts refined against observed performance.
05The stack

Built inside the client's own environment

Nothing here is a separate app the team has to remember to open. The build lives where the work already happens: the tables, flows, and documents the BD team touches every day.

06Outcome

The roadmap shipped, and the measurement shipped with it

Every phase on the roadmap was delivered, along with work beyond it. Triggers that previously surfaced weeks late are now captured as records with an owner and a deadline. Outreach that used to start from a blank page, or from a chatbot window nobody could audit, now starts from a reviewed draft built on stored, structured records.

7 / 7
phases on the roadmap, delivered

The last phase matters most: the system tracks automation adoption, follow-up timing, draft usage and edit rates, and conversion outcomes, so the rules, templates, and prompts get refined against observed performance rather than opinion.

I am not quoting time-saved or conversion numbers here. Those are the client's numbers to watch, on the instrumentation this build put in place. The claim I can stand behind is the mechanism: an event cannot silently expire, and nothing sends without a human saying send.
