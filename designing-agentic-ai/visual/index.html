Agent topologies and the shape of the work

Most leaders bolt agents onto the workflow they already run. A faster version of today's process. The bigger move is to redesign the shape of the work itself, and the shapes are not exotic. Scroll, and watch one troupe of agents rewire into every structure your organization already runs on.

A spatial companion to Designing Agentic Systems · Hamza Malik
Where the idea starts

One call center. Two moves.

A leader comes home from an agentic AI summit and looks at his floor with new eyes. The reflex is to automate the workflow he already has. Same shape, just faster. Safe, and the smaller prize.

The reframe is to redesign how the work is structured around what agents are good at. Which tasks collapse, who decides what, where the humans sit. Harder to do, and where the gains compound.

Everything that follows is one inbound call, shaped five different ways.

Shape 01 · Hub and spoke

One coordinator, a ring of specialists

The orchestrator holds the conversation and dispatches to narrow workers, each in its own isolated context. They report back, it composes the answer. The shape Anthropic used for its research product.

+Fast. Workers run at once, and you add or drop one as the work changes. −Nothing checks the workers, so one wrong answer can pass straight through.
In your organizationThe delegating manager. Parcels pieces to specialists who never talk to each other, assembles the result, and is the one set of eyes that has to catch every mistake.
Shape 02 · Pyramid

Layers, and a loop that sends work back

A wide base does the legwork. Reviewers above check the draft and send it back when it is not good enough. One sign-off at the top commits. The send-back loop is the mechanism doing the work: nothing ships unchecked.

+Catches mistakes. Every level reviews before anything commits. −Slow and costly. Every answer waits on a sign-off.
In your organizationThe firm with layers of review. Associate drafts, manager sends it back until it holds, partner signs. Quality bought with layers, worth it when being wrong is expensive.
Shape 03 · Pipeline

An assembly line with clean gates

Each stage feeds the next in fixed order. Every handoff is a gate you can check, log, or stop, which makes this the easiest shape to audit. Good for work that genuinely has to happen in order.

+Predictable and easy to govern. Each step is a clean, gated handoff. −Slow, and the whole chain stalls if any one step jams.
In your organizationThe standard operating procedure. Each desk does its step and passes the file along. The claims process, the mortgage pipeline, the assembly line.
Shape 04 · Parallel

Everything at once, merged at the end

Independent agents run at the same moment on parts that do not depend on each other, and the results merge at the end. The whole bet is independence. It turns dangerous when the parts secretly depend on each other.

+The fastest shape. Independent reads run at the same moment. −You have to reconcile results that come back not lining up.
In your organizationThe task force. Several people take independent angles at once, then converge. A war room, a due-diligence sprint, and the same merge headache.
Shape 05 · Mesh

Peers negotiating, no one in the middle

Agents talk straight to each other with no coordinator. Useful when specialists genuinely need to negotiate. Without someone deciding when the job is done, you build in a rule for when to stop, and you keep a supervisor watching.

+Flexible and powerful. Agents coordinate directly, with no bottleneck. −Hard to govern. Use rarely, and never unwatched.
In your organizationThe flat room of peers. No boss in the room, specialists negotiating directly. A trading desk, a founding team. Creative, quick, hard to govern.

No best shape

There is only the right shape for this call
